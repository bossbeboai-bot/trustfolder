## What this notice covers

{{ product_name }} can generate or manipulate {{ deepfake_type }} content that resembles real or realistic persons. Under Article 50(4) of the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), content of this nature ("deep fakes") must be disclosed to viewers.

## What is a "deep fake" under the EU AI Act

The EU AI Act defines a deep fake as AI-generated or manipulated image, audio, or video content that resembles existing persons, objects, places, entities or events and would falsely appear to a person to be authentic or truthful.

## Disclosure required

Whenever {{ product_name }} is used to generate deepfake content that is shared, published, or otherwise distributed, the deployer must disclose that the content has been artificially generated or manipulated.

The disclosure must be:
- Clear and distinguishable
- Provided at the time of first interaction or exposure
- Provided in a language understood by the audience

## How {{ product_name }} marks deepfake content

### Human-readable indicators
- Output files include a "Synthetic content — generated by AI" watermark or label
- Where output is delivered through an interface, an "AI-generated" tag is shown
- Documentation provided with each output explains the synthetic nature

### Machine-readable indicators

## Legal context to consider beyond the AI Act

Depending on jurisdiction and use case, the following frameworks may also apply to deepfake content:
- **GDPR:** Personal data processing (the depicted person's likeness is personal data)
- **Copyright / personality rights:** National laws on rights of publicity, image rights
- **Defamation:** National defamation/libel laws
- **Criminal law:** Sexual offences law, fraud, identity theft
- **National deepfake-specific laws:** Several EU member states have introduced or are considering specific deepfake legislation
