# Outbound Message Bank

## Purpose

This document defines the outreach copy used during Phase 4 Buyer Validation.

The goal is not to maximize replies. The goal is to learn which framing makes international B2B AI SaaS companies and AI agencies recognize the buyer-trust pain, accept the manual workflow, and engage with the free check or paid pack offer.

All messages are sent manually by the founder. No sequencing tools, no automation, no warm-up infrastructure are added in Phase 4.

## Operating Principles

- One sender identity. One inbox. Manual sends only.
- Personalization comes from the lead sheet trust gap signal.
- The free check is the primary call to action. Paid pack is offered only after engagement.
- No claims about scoring, certification, audits, or pass/fail outcomes.
- No aggressive scarcity, no fake urgency, no fabricated case studies.
- Plain text only. No tracked links in the first message.

## Message Channels in Scope

- Cold email to a verified work address.
- LinkedIn connection request plus a short follow-up note after acceptance.
- Reply-to-thread on a public post where the company or operator already discussed buyer trust, security questions, or procurement friction.

Out of scope for Phase 4:

- Bulk sequencing, mailmerge sends, or any tool that exceeds personal-mailbox volume.
- Paid amplification.
- Cold calls.
- WhatsApp or SMS outreach.
- Free trial spam to product signup forms.

## Section A · Cold Email Templates

Each template is one variant per angle. Variants are not stacked. Each lead receives one initial message and at most two follow-ups.

### A1 · Trust gap angle (primary)

Subject options:

- Quick note on your security page
- Buyer trust question for {company}
- Compliance friction in your enterprise deals

Body:

```
Hi {first name},

I run TrustFolder, a small operation that helps international AI SaaS teams
package the trust evidence buyers ask for during procurement.

I looked at {company}'s site and noticed the security and compliance section
is light on specifics. That usually shows up as drag in mid-market and
enterprise deals when the buyer's security team starts asking questions.

I am running a small free check this month. I look at your public trust
surface and send back a one-page note on what an AI-aware buyer would
likely flag. No deck, no automation, no obligation.

Want me to run one for {company}?

— {founder first name}
TrustFolder
```

Use when: the trust gap signal in the lead sheet was specifically a missing security page, missing AI policy, or weak data handling explanation.

## Section B · LinkedIn Templates

LinkedIn outreach is light touch. Connection request plus one follow-up after acceptance. No connection request without a reason.

### B1 · Connection request

Body limit: 280 characters.

```
Hi {first name}, I run TrustFolder. I help AI SaaS and agency teams
package the trust evidence buyers ask for in procurement. I would like
to connect and share a quick free check on {company}'s trust surface
if useful.
```

### B2 · Post-acceptance message

Body:

```
Thanks for connecting, {first name}.

I am running a small free check this month for international AI SaaS
and agency teams. I look at the public trust surface and send a one-page
note on what an AI-aware buyer is likely to ask about. Manual, no
automation, no obligation.

Want me to run one for {company}?

— {founder first name}
```

Use when: the connection request is accepted within seven days. If accepted later, switch to follow-up cadence in Section D.

### B3 · Reply-to-post

Used when the lead has publicly discussed buyer security questions, procurement friction, AI governance, or trust-related sales drag.

```
Adding context from a small operation that does this manually for AI
SaaS and agency teams. Most of the friction sits in the gap between what
buyers ask and what the public trust surface shows. I am offering a free
first-pass review this month if useful — happy to send the format
privately.
```

Use when: the post is on LinkedIn or X. Public reply only when the post is unambiguously about buyer trust friction.

Constraints:

## Section D · Follow-up Cadence

Maximum two follow-ups per lead. After the second follow-up, lead is closed unless they reply.

### D1 · First follow-up (T+5 business days)

```
Hi {first name},

Quick bump on the free check note I offered for {company}. Still happy
to run one if useful.

— {founder first name}
```

### D2 · Second follow-up (T+12 business days)

```
Hi {first name},

Last note from me on this. If buyer trust friction is not on the radar
right now, no problem. I will leave the offer open and stop following
up.

— {founder first name}
```

### D3 · Close-out

After the second follow-up, the lead is moved to a closed state in the lead sheet with reason `no response`.

No further outreach for at least 90 days unless a clear new trigger appears, such as a fundraising announcement, a new security page, or a public buyer-trust complaint.

## Section E · Objection Handlers

Used in reply threads only. Each handler is a single short paragraph.

### E1 · "We already have a security page"

```
Totally fair. The free check looks at how an AI-aware buyer is likely to
read your public trust surface, not whether a page exists. The difference
between having a page and having a page that clears procurement on the
first pass is usually where the friction sits. Happy to send the note if
useful.
```

### E2 · "We use a third-party trust portal already"

```
Understood. The free check is not a replacement for a trust portal. It
focuses on the public-facing version of your trust surface, which is what
a buyer sees before they get a portal link. Often the gap is between
those two layers. No portal access required.
```

### E3 · "We are not looking to spend right now"

```
No spend involved in the free check. It is a one-page note, manually
written, no automation. The paid pack is only relevant if the note
points to something worth packaging.
```

### E4 · "Send me a deck or one-pager"

```
No deck. The free check itself is the one-pager. If it is useful, the
paid pack is a fixed-scope manual deliverable. I prefer to keep the
artifact concrete instead of marketing material.
```

### E5 · "How is this different from {SOC 2 vendor}?"

```
Different layer. SOC 2 vendors handle audit, evidence collection, and
controls. TrustFolder packages the buyer-facing answer to what the
procurement team will ask before they ever see the SOC 2 report. We sit
upstream of that workflow, not on top of it.
```

### E6 · "Is this AI generated?"

```
The note is written manually by the founder. The paid pack is also
written and assembled manually. The product is the human review and
packaging, not the automation.
```

### E7 · "Send me references"

```
Phase one is small and recent, so no public references yet. Happy to
share the format and a redacted sample of a free check if useful.
```

## Section F · Hard Rules

The following must not appear in any outreach message under any circumstances during Phase 4.

- Claims of being SOC 2 certified, ISO certified, audited, or accredited.
- Claims of guaranteed deal closure, guaranteed score improvements, or guaranteed compliance pass.
- Use of the words "audit", "certification", or "compliance grade" to describe TrustFolder's own output.
- References to clients TrustFolder does not have.
- Logos or names of companies TrustFolder has not delivered for.
- Fake urgency, expiring discounts, or invented scarcity.
- Auto-generated personalization that names the wrong product, role, or company.
- Tracking pixels in the first message.
- Bulk sender infrastructure or warm-up tools.

## Section G · Test and Variant Plan

The 14-day window in `25-phase-4-buyer-validation-plan.md` defines the validation budget. The message bank is tested inside that window with the following constraints.

- Each angle (A1, A2, A3, A4) is sent to a roughly equal slice of the lead list.
- Subject lines are rotated within each angle, not across angles.
- LinkedIn outreach (B1+B2) is run in parallel for a subset of leads where LinkedIn is the stronger channel.
- Public-reply outreach (B3) is opportunistic and not scheduled.
- After 14 days the founder records reply rate, free-check acceptance rate, paid-pack request rate, and qualitative reasons for refusal per angle.

The output of this test is read into the validation scorecard in `29-validation-scorecard.md`.

## Section H · Out of Scope

Phase 4 does not include:

- Drip sequences beyond two follow-ups.
- Automated personalization tooling.
- Multi-touch, multi-channel sequencing software.
- Paid ads.
- Outbound dialers or call automation.
- Branded landing pages built specifically for outbound.
- New email domains, warm-up tools, or dedicated outreach infrastructure.

If outreach volume becomes the bottleneck, the correct response in Phase 4 is to narrow the lead list, not to add infrastructure.
