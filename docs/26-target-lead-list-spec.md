# Target Lead List Spec

## Purpose

This document defines the lead sourcing criteria for Phase 4 Buyer Validation.

The goal is to build a small, high-quality list of international B2B AI SaaS companies and AI agencies that are likely to feel buyer-trust friction but may not yet have mature compliance infrastructure.

This is not a growth list. It is a validation list.

## Ideal Customer Profile

### Primary ICP: International B2B AI SaaS

Companies that sell AI-powered software to business buyers and need to appear credible during buyer review.

Strong examples:

- **AI customer support platforms**.
- **AI workflow automation tools**.
- **AI data extraction and enrichment tools**.
- **AI analytics or BI copilots**.
- **AI sales or customer success copilots**.
- **AI HR, recruiting, or people-ops tools**.
- **Vertical AI tools for business workflows**.
- **AI document processing products**.

Required signs:

- Business-facing website.
- Clear product offer.
- Some evidence of commercial motion.
- Public buyer-facing claims.
- Likely handling of customer data, documents, prompts, or workflow information.
- Missing or incomplete trust/security/compliance explanation.

### Secondary ICP: AI Agencies and Automation Studios

Service businesses building AI workflows for clients.

Strong examples:

- **AI automation agencies** building internal tools.
- **Chatbot implementation studios**.
- **AI workflow consultants**.
- **Custom agent development agencies**.
- **No-code/low-code AI automation shops**.
- **Vertical AI implementation partners**.

Required signs:

- Business clients, not consumers.
- Case studies or client-facing service pages.
- Claims around automation, agents, data, integrations, or operational workflows.
- Likely exposure to client security questions.
- No clear trust pack, AI governance page, data handling explanation, or procurement-ready material.

## Geographic Priority

Prioritize international companies where trust readiness may be a sales differentiator.

High-priority regions:

- United Kingdom.
- European Union.
- Canada.
- Australia and New Zealand.
- Singapore.
- United Arab Emirates.
- India.
- Israel.
- South Africa.
- LATAM companies selling to US/EU buyers.

Do not exclude US companies entirely, but Phase 4 should avoid becoming a generic US compliance-platform validation exercise.

## Company Stage Criteria

Best-fit companies are past idea stage but not fully compliance-mature.

### Include

- Launched product with active website.
- Visible demo, pricing, case studies, customer logos, testimonials, or founder-led sales motion.
- 2-50 person teams.
- Recently launched AI product line.
- Agency with repeatable AI service offer.
- Company selling to SMB, mid-market, or enterprise buyers.
- Company likely to face questionnaires before closing deals.

### Avoid

- No live product.
- No B2B buyer.
- Pure consumer app.
- Large enterprise vendor with mature security team.
- Company already showing a mature trust center.
- Company primarily selling regulated legal, medical, or financial compliance where TrustFolder would be too shallow.
- Company with unclear ownership, no contact route, or no evidence of activity.

## Trust Gap Signals

Prioritize leads with visible trust gaps.

Strong gap signals:

- No security page.
- No privacy page or weak privacy page.
- No AI governance explanation.
- No data retention explanation.
- No subprocessors list.
- No DPA mention.
- No customer data handling page.
- No enterprise/procurement materials.
- No FAQ for security or compliance.
- Strong AI claims without supporting trust material.
- Agency claiming client data automation without explaining safeguards.
- Product handles documents, tickets, emails, CRM, HR, finance, or customer support data.

Moderate gap signals:

- Has privacy policy but no security explanation.
- Has security claims but no buyer-ready summary.
- Has compliance logos without context.
- Has docs but no sales-facing trust pack.
- Has AI safety language but no procurement-facing artifact.

Disqualifying maturity signals:

- Full trust center.
- SOC 2 report request flow.
- ISO 27001 certificate.
- Detailed DPA and subprocessors page.
- Security whitepaper.
- Pen test summary.
- Mature vendor security portal.

A mature trust center does not always disqualify a lead, but it reduces the value of Phase 4 outreach unless there is a clear agency/client packaging angle.

## Lead Qualification Score

Score each lead from 0-10 before outreach.

### Scoring Rubric

- **B2B relevance, 0-2**: Does the company clearly sell to business buyers?
- **AI/data sensitivity, 0-2**: Does the product likely touch customer data, documents, workflows, or AI outputs?
- **Trust gap visibility, 0-2**: Are trust/security/compliance materials missing or weak?
- **Commercial maturity, 0-2**: Is there enough traction or sales motion to care now?
- **Contactability, 0-2**: Is there a founder, sales, partnerships, or ops contact reachable?

### Outreach Priority

- **8-10**: Priority outreach.
- **6-7**: Good backup lead.
- **4-5**: Keep only if there is a strong personalization angle.
- **0-3**: Do not contact during Phase 4.

## Required Lead Sheet Fields

Use a spreadsheet or CRM-like table with these fields.

### Company Fields

- Company name.
- Website URL.
- Country or region.
- Company type.
- Segment.
- Product description.
- Buyer type.
- Team size estimate.
- Stage signal.
- Pricing visible.
- Customer logos or case studies visible.

### Trust Gap Fields

- Security page present.
- Privacy page present.
- DPA present.
- Subprocessors listed.
- AI governance page present.
- Trust center present.
- Trust gap summary.
- Public risk note.
- Strongest buyer-friction hypothesis.

### Contact Fields

- Contact name.
- Contact role.
- Contact source.
- Contact channel.
- Email or LinkedIn URL.
- Founder-led or team-led.
- Personalization hook.

### Outreach Fields

- Qualification score.
- Outreach priority.
- Message variant.
- Date contacted.
- Follow-up date.
- Reply status.
- Response tag.
- Free check requested.
- Paid intent signal.
- Notes.

## Recommended Contact Roles

Prioritize people who feel buyer friction directly.

Best contacts:

- Founder or co-founder.
- CEO.
- COO.
- Head of Sales.
- Head of Growth.
- Head of Partnerships.
- RevOps lead.
- Customer success lead.
- Agency founder.
- Delivery lead at AI agency.

Avoid as first contact:

- Generic support inbox if a founder route exists.
- Engineers with no commercial ownership.
- HR or recruiting contacts.
- Investors.
- Junior marketers unless they own sales collateral.

## Sourcing Channels

Use manual sourcing. Do not depend on scraping automation for Phase 4.

Recommended channels:

- Product Hunt AI launches.
- LinkedIn search for AI SaaS founders and AI agency founders.
- AI tool directories.
- Startup directories.
- Indie Hackers or founder communities.
- Agency directories.
- Clutch-style agency listings.
- Recent AI launch posts.
- Founder newsletters and launch announcements.
- Search queries around specific verticals.

Example search queries:

- `site:linkedin.com/in founder "AI automation agency" UK`
- `"AI customer support" "security" "privacy" SaaS`
- `"AI workflow automation" "case studies" agency`
- `"AI document processing" SaaS "privacy policy"`
- `"AI copilot" "for teams" "pricing"`
- `"AI agents" agency "clients"`

## Personalization Angles

Every contacted lead should have a specific reason for outreach.

Good angles:

- Their product handles sensitive customer documents.
- Their website has strong AI claims but no security explanation.
- Their agency sells client automations without a visible trust artifact.
- Their product likely receives procurement/security questions.
- Their trust materials are scattered and not buyer-ready.
- Their pricing suggests B2B buyers but trust collateral is thin.
- Their case studies imply enterprise or mid-market buyers.

Weak angles:

- Generic AI company.
- Recently launched.
- Looks interesting.
- Might need compliance.
- Similar to other tools.

## Lead Exclusion Rules

Do not contact leads that create avoidable risk or poor validation quality.

Exclude if:

- There is no clear B2B use case.
- The website is inactive or placeholder-only.
- The company already has mature trust collateral.
- The company is obviously too large or compliance-mature.
- The product is consumer entertainment, image generation, or personal productivity without business data risk.
- The likely need is legal counsel, regulatory certification, SOC 2 audit, ISO implementation, or penetration testing.
- The company would require new TrustFolder features to serve safely.

## Minimum Lead Batch

For the 14-day validation sprint, prepare:

- **60 qualified leads minimum**.
- **80 preferred**.
- **40 B2B AI SaaS minimum**.
- **20 AI agencies minimum**.
- **At least 5 countries represented**.
- **At least 30 leads scoring 8+**.

## Quality Bar

A lead is usable only if the sender can complete this sentence:

> I am contacting this company because they sell [product/service] to [buyer], appear likely to face [buyer-trust friction], and currently lack [specific trust material].

If that sentence cannot be completed, do not include the lead in the first Phase 4 batch.
