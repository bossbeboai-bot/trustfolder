# 01 · Brand Language Rules · Do/Don't List

The single canonical reference for what words to use (and not use) in every customer-facing surface: website copy, emails, template footers, social posts, support replies, advisor pitch, sales DMs.

**Why this exists:** "Compliance" is a legally loaded word. If our marketing says "compliance" and our disclaimer says "not legal advice," the contradiction destroys trust. This doc keeps everyone (including the AI engine writing template content) aligned on safe language.

---

## The single rule

> We sell **preparation**, not compliance.  
> We sell **readiness**, not certification.  
> We sell **time saved**, not legal protection.

If a phrase implies any of the latter three, rewrite it.

## DO say

### Product positioning
- "AI Governance Readiness Pack"
- "First-pass readiness materials"
- "Lawyer-review-ready documentation"
- "Reduce preparation time before legal review"
- "Generated from advisor-reviewed templates"
- "Structured from official EU AI Act obligations"
- "AI governance documentation starter"
- "Helps you organize AI governance materials"
- "Designed for small AI SaaS companies preparing for EU customers"

### Outcomes
- "Save 20+ hours of legal preparation time"
- "Hand a structured pack to your lawyer instead of starting from scratch"
- "Answer enterprise vendor questionnaires faster"
- "Get organized before your next compliance conversation"

### Disclaimers (use everywhere)
- "Not legal advice, certification, or guaranteed compliance."
- "This toolkit helps you organize AI governance materials and prepare for expert review."
- "Documents are AI-generated drafts. Review with your legal counsel before publication."
- "Not a substitute for qualified legal or compliance advice."

### Confidence-band language
- "Likely limited-risk transparency obligation"
- "Requires legal/compliance review"
- "Cannot classify from current data"
- "Out of automated scope — please contact a qualified expert"

## DO NOT say

### Forbidden compliance claims
- ❌ "Become EU AI Act compliant in 24 hours"
- ❌ "Fully compliant"
- ❌ "ISO 42001 certified documents"
- ❌ "Certification ready"
- ❌ "Audit-proof"
- ❌ "Regulator-ready"
- ❌ "Pass any compliance audit"
- ❌ "Guaranteed compliance"

### Forbidden expertise claims
- ❌ "AI is the compliance expert"
- ❌ "No lawyer needed"
- ❌ "Replace your compliance officer"
- ❌ "Skip legal review"
- ❌ "100% autonomous compliance"
- ❌ "Built by compliance experts" (unless we have one — and even then, "advisor-reviewed" is safer)

### Forbidden fear-marketing
- ❌ "Avoid €35M fines"
- ❌ "Don't get fined"
- ❌ "Fines guaranteed if you don't act now"
- ❌ Single-deadline countdown timers as primary CTA

### Forbidden over-claims
- ❌ "Fully autonomous compliance"
- ❌ "Complete compliance solution"
- ❌ "Everything you need for AI Act"
- ❌ "Legal-grade documentation"

## Footer / disclaimer copy (canonical)

Use this exact wording (or close paraphrase) on every page footer and every delivered document:

> **Disclaimer.** This toolkit and its outputs are provided for informational and preparatory purposes only. They are AI-generated drafts based on advisor-reviewed templates and the information you provide. They do not constitute legal advice, certification, or guaranteed compliance with any regulation including the EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 42001, GDPR, or other applicable laws. Always review outputs with qualified legal counsel before publication or submission to regulators, customers, or auditors. Regulatory requirements change — see our regulatory-watch page for current status.

## Tone rules

- **Calm, not urgent.** Avoid countdown timers, "ACT NOW" language, fine-fear.
- **Practical, not legal.** Talk about workflows and time saved, not regulations and penalties.
- **Honest, not hyped.** "First pass" and "starter" are positives — they signal we know our limits.
- **Helpful, not authoritative.** "Your lawyer can use this as a starting point" beats "Use this as your compliance solution."

---

## When in doubt, ask

> "Would I be comfortable saying this in a courtroom under oath?"

If no → rewrite.

> "Would a sophisticated buyer roll their eyes at this?"

If yes → tone it down.

> "If a regulator screenshot this, would I have to apologize?"

If yes → kill it.
