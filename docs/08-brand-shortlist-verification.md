## Detail · 5 original candidates

### 1. PolicyPack ❌ HARD ELIMINATE

**Why it fails:**
- **PolicyPak** (now branded as Netwrix Endpoint Policy Manager) is a long-established Windows desktop/policy management product
- policypak.com is an active SaaS product page
- G2, ZoomInfo, Superbcrew all index PolicyPak as the established brand
- "PolicyPack" vs "PolicyPak" is one-letter-different — guaranteed customer confusion + likely cease & desist if you grow
- Buyer search for "policy pack" returns PolicyPak as the top result

**Verdict:** Do not use.

### 2. ProofKit ❌ HARD ELIMINATE

**Why it fails:**
- **proofkit.dev** — FileMaker development toolset by Proof+Geist (active product)
- **proofkit.proof.sh** — FileMaker Web Viewer toolset (active product)
- **proofkit.net** — "ProofKit generates evidence packages with automatic tampering detection that stand up to regulatory scrutiny" — **literally in the compliance/evidence product space**
- **github.com/proofgeist/proofkit** — established public repo with community
- proofkit.net's positioning is essentially a competitor to the readiness pack concept

**Verdict:** Do not use. The .net is the killer — it's the same wedge.

### 3. RegReady ❌ HARD ELIMINATE

**Why it fails:**
- **youtube.com/regready** is an existing brand: "RegReady is a transparent, fair and balanced customer acquisition community helping marketers to develop true opt-in marketing lists"
- Different category (lead generation vs compliance) but the brand exists with active web presence
- Even if no trademark conflict, the SEO landscape is already shaped

**Verdict:** Do not use.

### 4. ActReady ⚠ MEDIUM-HIGH RISK

**Why it's risky:**
- No direct "ActReady" brand found in web search
- BUT: **Act! CRM** (Act! LLC, formerly Symantec/Sage) is a 30+ year-established CRM brand
- "Act" prefix in software = Act! brand association in many buyers' minds
- Company name "Act!" + variations (Act! Pro, Act! Premium, Act! Cloud) heavily occupy the prefix
- Confusion risk = customers searching "Act ready" land on Act! CRM materials

**Possible mitigations:**
- USPTO/EUIPO trademark search to confirm no Class 9 / Class 42 conflict before locking
- Trademark Class 9 (software) is the relevant class — Act! has strong claims there

**Verdict:** Defer pending USPTO/EUIPO trademark check. Not eliminated but not recommended without further verification.

### 5. TrustFolder ✓ LOW-MEDIUM RISK (cleanest of original 5)

**Why it's viable:**
- No direct "TrustFolder" brand found in web search
- **Brandfolder** (Smartsheet-owned, digital asset management) exists with similar suffix structure
- Different product space (DAM vs compliance), so brand-confusion risk is mild
- "trustfolders.com" appears in scamadviser results — not a real brand, just generic
- Trust + Folder = clean evidence/compliance metaphor (folder of trust documents)

**Mild concern:**
- "Trust" prefix is heavily used (TrustRadius, Trustpilot, TrustBase, Trustible, Trust company)
- Differentiation in marketing required

**Verdict:** Viable. Strongest of original 5.

## Detail · 2 added alternatives

### 6. ComplyAhead ⭐ STRONG ALTERNATIVE

**Why it's clean:**
- Web search returns no direct brand match
- "Comply" prefix is used (Compliance.ai, ComplyAdvantage) but "Ahead" suffix unique
- Implies forward-looking preparation — aligns with "readiness, not compliance" positioning
- Two-syllable per word, easy to pronounce
- Available as a verb-phrase: "comply ahead of regulation"

**Mild concern:**
- ComplyAdvantage (financial crime risk platform, well-funded) shares "Comply" prefix
- Naming similarity is moderate but product space is different (AML/KYC vs AI Act readiness)

**Verdict:** Strong candidate.

### 7. ReadinessKit ⭐ STRONG ALTERNATIVE

**Why it's clean:**
- Web search returns no direct brand match
- "Readiness" + "Kit" = exactly what the product is — no metaphor distance
- Domain availability likely high (long compound word)
- Clear product framing that supports the "preparation, not compliance" positioning
- Generic enough to extend to other readiness verticals (SOC 2, GDPR readiness) later

**Mild concern:**
- Generic compound — harder to trademark strongly (descriptive marks are weaker)
- Less brandable than a coined word

**Verdict:** Strong candidate, especially for SEO-driven acquisition.
