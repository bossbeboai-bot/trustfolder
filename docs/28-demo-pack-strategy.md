# Demo Pack Strategy

## Purpose

This document defines the artifacts used during Phase 4 Buyer Validation to demonstrate what TrustFolder delivers, without building new product surface area.

The demo pack is the manual deliverable. It is the thing a buyer reviews to decide whether the free check converts into a paid pack request. Phase 4 succeeds or fails on whether qualified buyers find this artifact credible enough to pay for.

## Phase 4 Operating Principle

The demo pack is delivered manually. No automation, no dashboard, no portal, no auto-generated PDFs.

The founder produces every demo pack and free check personally during Phase 4. Templates are used as scaffolds, not as a generator.

Explicitly out of scope:

- Self-serve demo flows.
- Dashboard previews.
- Logged-in demo environments.
- Auto-generated trust packs.
- New connectors, integrations, or auto-fetch of third-party data.

## Two Artifacts in Phase 4

Phase 4 has two manual artifacts. Nothing else.

### Artifact 1 · Free Check Note

A one-page written note delivered by email after a lead opts in.

Purpose: Show the lead that TrustFolder understands their public trust surface and can identify what an AI-aware buyer would ask next.

Length: One page. Roughly 300 to 500 words.

Format: Plain text or lightly formatted PDF. No interactive elements.

Delivery: Sent as an email body or attached as a single PDF.

Source of content: The founder reviews the lead's public trust surface manually. No scrapers, no scoring engine, no scoring claim.

Constraints:

- No score, grade, or pass/fail label.
- No claim of certification or audit.
- No automation.
- No links to a dashboard or login.
- No upsell beyond a single mention of the paid pack option.

### Artifact 2 · Paid Trust Pack

A multi-section written pack delivered after a lead pays the agreed fee.

Purpose: Give the buyer's procurement and security reviewer everything they need to clear the file on the first pass.

Length: Five to ten pages, depending on the buyer's product surface.

Format: PDF with a written narrative. No interactive components.

Delivery: Sent as a single PDF over email after payment is confirmed.

Source of content: Founder writes and assembles each section manually based on the lead's public material, the founder's review, and any clarifying questions answered by the buyer.

Constraints:

- No claim that the pack is an audit.
- No claim that the pack guarantees a procurement outcome.
- No use of certification language.
- Manual delivery only.
- Fixed scope. Out-of-scope additions become a follow-up engagement.

## Free Check Note · Required Sections

The note has four sections, in order. Length and tone are conversational, not legalistic.

### Section A · What I looked at

Two to four lines naming the public surfaces reviewed.

Examples of surfaces:

- Security or trust page.
- Privacy policy.
- AI policy or model use language.
- Sub-processor list.
- Contact path for security questions.
- Visible compliance signals such as SOC 2 logos or trust portals.

### Section B · What I noticed

Two to four observations grounded in what is publicly visible.

Each observation must be specific. No generic statements such as "your trust page could be stronger." Every observation must reference what is actually present or missing.

### Section C · What an AI-aware buyer would ask next

Three to five concrete questions a procurement reviewer would raise.

These are written from the buyer's perspective, not as marketing copy.

### Section D · Optional next step

One short paragraph offering the paid pack at the agreed price.

This section must be short, must not pressure the buyer, and must clarify that the free check is useful regardless of whether they request the paid pack.

## Paid Trust Pack · Required Sections

The paid pack has six fixed sections. Section length depends on the buyer's product, but the structure is constant.

### Section 1 · Product and buyer context

A short narrative describing what the company does, who its buyers are, and which procurement scenarios this pack is intended to support.

This section is written based on the founder's review of public material plus any short intake conversation with the lead.

### Section 2 · Data handling explanation

A description of:

- What data the company processes.
- Where it is processed.
- How it is stored.
- How long it is retained.
- Who has access internally.

Written from public material, the company's own statements, and clarifying answers when needed. Where information is missing, the section explicitly says so rather than inventing detail.

### Section 3 · AI use and model governance

A description of:

- Which AI models the product uses.
- Whether buyer data is used for training.
- What guardrails are in place.
- How the company handles model updates and failure modes.
- Any human-in-the-loop steps.

This section is the most differentiated part of the pack and the section where most public trust surfaces are weakest.

### Section 4 · Sub-processors and third-party stack

A list of third-party services that touch buyer data, with a short explanation of each.

If the buyer asks for an updated list later, the founder issues a follow-up note. No live sub-processor feed is implied.

### Section 5 · Security posture

A narrative description of:

- Authentication.
- Access control.
- Logging and monitoring.
- Incident response process.
- Recovery and continuity.

Written in plain language. No fabrication. Where the company has weak controls, the section names them honestly with proposed mitigations.

### Section 6 · Buyer FAQ

A pre-answered set of questions a procurement reviewer is most likely to ask.

This section is written so that the buyer's security team can copy and paste answers into their internal review template without follow-up.

## Format Standards

The demo pack must look credible without looking generated.

- Single column.
- Readable line length.
- One typeface.
- No stock graphics.
- No score widgets.
- No badges that imply certification.
- No watermark or branding that overshadows the buyer's name.

The pack is the product of careful writing, not visual styling.

## Personalization Rules

Every demo pack is personalized to the buyer.

- Company name appears on the cover.
- Product description matches the company's actual public language.
- Sub-processors are inferred only when publicly stated. Otherwise marked as "to be confirmed."
- Compliance frameworks named are only those the company actually claims.

If the founder cannot confidently personalize a section, the section is marked "to be confirmed in intake" rather than guessed.

## Sample Library

The founder maintains a small private sample library of three redacted examples to share when a lead asks "what does the pack actually look like."

The library is private. It is not published on the public site during Phase 4.

Rules for samples:

- Always redacted.
- Always with explicit permission from the original buyer.
- Never used as a marketing testimonial.

If no shareable redacted sample exists yet, the founder shares a structural outline only.

## Pricing Communication

Phase 4 uses a single fixed price for the paid pack and a single free check.

- Free check: zero cost, one per lead.
- Paid pack: a fixed price decided before Phase 4 begins.

No tiered pricing. No bundles. No discounts.

If a lead requests a custom scope, the response is to acknowledge it and either:

- Decline and refer them to the standard pack.
- Note it as a Phase 5 candidate.

No bespoke quotes are issued during Phase 4.

## Delivery Workflow

The full delivery workflow is manual. The founder is the only operator.

### Free check workflow

1. Lead opts in via email reply or LinkedIn message.
2. Founder reviews public trust surface.
3. Founder writes the four-section note.
4. Founder sends the note as a single email or PDF attachment.
5. Founder logs the lead and the angle that converted in the lead sheet.

Target turnaround: same day or next business day.

### Paid pack workflow

1. Lead requests the paid pack.
2. Founder confirms scope and price by email.
3. Lead pays via the agreed manual payment path (PayPal where available, otherwise the existing public site flow).
4. Founder runs a short intake conversation if needed.
5. Founder writes and assembles the six-section pack.
6. Founder delivers the pack as a single PDF.
7. Founder logs the order and outcome in the lead sheet.

Target turnaround: three to five business days from payment.

## Quality Bar

A paid pack is not delivered until it meets these conditions.

- Every section is present.
- Every claim is grounded in public material or buyer-confirmed information.
- Where information is missing, the pack says so plainly.
- No section is filler.
- The pack reads like a procurement reviewer can clear the file with it.

The founder reads the pack end to end before sending. If a section feels weak, it is rewritten before delivery.

## Refunds and Recovery

If a paid pack does not meet the buyer's expectation, the founder offers:

- A single revision pass at no extra cost.
- A full refund if the buyer is not satisfied after the revision.

The refund offer must be honored without dispute during Phase 4. Refunds are signal, not failure.

If two consecutive paid packs end in refund, Phase 4 escalates to the kill or pivot review described in `25-phase-4-buyer-validation-plan.md`.

## Out of Scope for Phase 4

The following are deferred to a later phase regardless of buyer interest.

- Auto-generated packs.
- Live trust portal.
- Customer dashboard.
- Connector-based evidence collection.
- Subscription pricing.
- Re-issue automation.
- Magic-link authentication.
- New payment provider integrations.
- Per-paragraph inline source notes inside the pack.
- Public sample gallery.

Buyer requests for any of the above are recorded as Phase 5 demand signal in the validation scorecard, not built during Phase 4.
