# Phase 4 Buyer Validation Plan

## Purpose

Phase 4 validates whether TrustFolder has a real buyer pull from international B2B AI SaaS companies and AI agencies before adding any new product surface area.

The goal is not to scale acquisition, automate delivery, or build a dashboard. The goal is to learn whether qualified buyers understand the pain, trust the promise, accept the manual workflow, and show willingness to pay for a compliance credibility pack.

## Phase 4 Operating Principle

Do not build new product features during Phase 4.

Validation is run manually using the existing public site, request flow, email, PayPal-enabled paid tiers where available, and founder-operated delivery/rescue process.

Explicitly out of scope:

- **No auth**: Do not add account creation, login, magic links, or user portals.
- **No dashboard**: Do not build customer or admin dashboards.
- **No connectors**: Do not add Google Drive, Notion, Slack, CRM, Vanta, Drata, or repository integrations.
- **No subscriptions**: Do not introduce recurring billing or plan management.
- **No Remotion**: Do not add video generation or animated report production.
- **No new payment provider**: Do not add Stripe or replace PayPal during this phase.
- **No product automation expansion**: Do not build new self-serve features in response to early buyer feedback.

## Validation Hypotheses

Phase 4 tests five practical hypotheses.

- **Pain exists**: International B2B AI companies lose time, credibility, or deals because they cannot answer buyer security and compliance questions cleanly.
- **Urgency exists**: The problem is painful enough to trigger a reply, booked call, free check, or paid request within 14 days.
- **TrustFolder framing is clear**: Buyers understand the value of a lightweight buyer-facing trust pack without needing a full compliance platform.
- **Manual delivery is acceptable**: Early buyers tolerate founder-led checks and delivery if the output is useful and fast.
- **Paid intent exists**: At least some qualified leads are willing to pay for a Trust Pack or ask buying questions that imply budget.

## Target Segment

Primary validation segment:

- **International B2B AI SaaS companies** selling to mid-market or enterprise buyers.
- **AI agencies and automation studios** implementing AI workflows for business clients.
- **Companies outside the US-first enterprise compliance bubble** that still face buyer security reviews.

The strongest targets are teams with visible traction but incomplete buyer trust infrastructure.

Examples:

- **AI support tools**: Chatbots, helpdesk copilots, support automation.
- **AI workflow tools**: Internal automation, document workflows, operations copilots.
- **AI data tools**: Enrichment, analytics, extraction, classification, data processing.
- **AI agencies**: Custom automation, AI agents, internal tooling, chatbot implementation.
- **Vertical AI SaaS**: Legal, HR, recruiting, finance, healthcare-adjacent, real estate, logistics.

## Non-Target Segment

Avoid leads where TrustFolder is unlikely to be credible or useful at this stage.

- **Large enterprises** with established security/compliance teams.
- **Companies already using mature trust centers** with SOC 2, ISO 27001, DPA, subprocessors, pen test summaries, and security portals.
- **Consumer AI apps** without B2B sales motion.
- **Regulated-first products** where TrustFolder would be insufficient or risky as a compliance substitute.
- **Pre-product idea pages** with no clear buyer, website, or commercial motion.
- **Companies asking for legal certification** rather than buyer-readiness packaging.

## Validation Offer

The Phase 4 offer should remain simple.

### Free Check

A lightweight manual review of the company’s public trust readiness.

Positioning:

> I can do a quick free check of your public buyer-trust gaps and send back the 3-5 things most likely to slow down an enterprise or agency client review.

The free check should not be a full generated pack. It should be a concise manual email or note.

Deliverable:

- **3-5 buyer trust gaps** found from public materials.
- **One suggested quick win**.
- **One recommended pack type** if relevant.
- **Clear caveat** that this is not legal advice, certification, or formal compliance assessment.

### Paid Trust Pack

A founder-operated paid pack for qualified companies that need a buyer-facing artifact.

Positioning:

> If useful, I can turn this into a buyer-ready Trust Pack: a structured set of security, AI governance, privacy, and procurement-facing materials you can share during sales conversations.

The paid pack should use existing delivery mechanics and current product scope. If a request cannot be served safely, route it to manual follow-up or refund rather than building new functionality.

## 14-Day Validation Plan

### Day 0: Setup

Prepare the validation system before outreach begins.

Actions:

- **Create lead sheet** with fields from `docs/26-target-lead-list-spec.md`.
- **Prepare demo links or screenshots** using fictional companies from `docs/28-demo-pack-strategy.md`.
- **Prepare outreach snippets** from `docs/27-outbound-message-bank.md`.
- **Define response tags** from `docs/29-validation-scorecard.md`.
- **Confirm manual operating model** for free checks and paid requests.
- **Confirm no feature-building rule** for the full 14 days.

Exit condition:

- Lead tracker, message bank, demo material, and scorecard are ready.

### Days 1-2: Build First Lead Batch

Source 60-80 leads.

Target mix:

- **40 B2B AI SaaS companies**.
- **20 AI agencies or automation studios**.
- **Optional 20 backup leads** if reply quality is low.

Required fields:

- Company name.
- Website.
- Segment.
- Country or region.
- Buyer-facing offer.
- Evidence of B2B motion.
- Trust gap notes.
- Contact name.
- Contact role.
- Contact channel.
- Personalization angle.
- Qualification score.

Exit condition:

- At least 60 usable leads with contact routes and a clear reason to contact each.

### Days 3-5: First Outreach Wave

Send 30-40 high-personalization messages.

Rules:

- Keep messages short.
- Lead with a specific observed trust gap.
- Offer a free check, not a product pitch.
- Avoid compliance overclaiming.
- Avoid claiming certification, legal review, SOC 2 replacement, or automated compliance.
- Track every send and reply.

Daily volume:

- **Day 3**: 10-15 messages.
- **Day 4**: 10-15 messages.
- **Day 5**: 10-15 messages.

Exit condition:

- At least 30 first-touch messages sent.

### Days 6-7: Free Checks and Conversation Handling

Respond to interested leads manually.

Actions:

- Run free checks for qualified responders.
- Ask one diagnostic question if needed before reviewing.
- Send concise findings within 24-48 hours.
- Offer a paid Trust Pack only if the pain is real and the lead fits.
- Log objections, exact words, and next actions.

Exit condition:

- Every positive reply has a manual follow-up, free check, or scheduled next step.

### Days 8-9: Second Outreach Wave

Send another 30-40 messages using the strongest-performing angle from wave one.

Adjust only messaging, not product.

Possible angle shifts:

- From general compliance readiness to buyer security review friction.
- From AI governance to enterprise procurement readiness.
- From trust page gaps to sales-cycle acceleration.
- From agency credibility to client procurement support.

Exit condition:

- At least 60 total first-touch messages sent by end of Day 9.

### Days 10-11: Follow-Up Wave

Follow up with non-responders and warm responders.

Rules:

- Send one short follow-up per prospect.
- Add a specific observation or example.
- Do not guilt, pressure, or imply urgency artificially.
- Keep opt-out easy.

Exit condition:

- All qualified Day 3-5 non-responders receive one follow-up.

### Days 12-13: Paid Intent Push

Focus on leads showing problem awareness.

Actions:

- Ask whether they want a buyer-ready pack prepared.
- Offer a clear manual turnaround range.
- Clarify that the pack is not certification or legal advice.
- Route mismatched needs to manual decline or future waitlist.
- Track pricing objections and buying-language exactly.

Exit condition:

- All warm leads have been asked a concrete next-step question.

### Day 14: Score and Decide

Apply `docs/29-validation-scorecard.md`.

Assess:

- Reply rate.
- Positive reply rate.
- Free check requests.
- Call requests.
- Paid pack requests.
- Objection quality.
- Segment fit.
- Message clarity.
- Manual delivery feasibility.

Exit condition:

- Continue, narrow, pivot, or stop decision is documented.

## Manual Operating Model

### Intake

All interested responses are handled by the founder manually.

Accepted channels:

- Email reply.
- LinkedIn reply.
- Website request form.
- Direct referral.

For each response, log:

- Lead source.
- Original message variant.
- Company website.
- Segment.
- Buyer problem described.
- Requested next step.
- Fit assessment.
- Response tag.

### Free Check Workflow

Use this workflow for qualified free checks.

1. Review website, trust page, privacy page, terms, docs, pricing, and any public security materials.
2. Identify 3-5 concrete buyer-trust gaps.
3. Identify one fast credibility improvement.
4. Identify whether a paid Trust Pack would be relevant.
5. Send a concise email with findings.
6. Log whether the lead replies, asks a question, requests a call, or asks for pricing.

Time cap:

- **20-30 minutes per free check**.

Do not let free checks become unpaid consulting projects.

### Paid Pack Request Workflow

Use this workflow when a qualified lead asks for a paid pack.

1. Confirm company fit and package scope.
2. Clarify that TrustFolder creates buyer-facing readiness materials, not legal certification.
3. Confirm turnaround and payment route using existing payment/manual process.
4. Collect only the minimum needed materials.
5. Use current generation and packaging capabilities where applicable.
6. Manually QA the output before delivery.
7. Deliver by email using existing delivery flow or manual secure link.
8. Log outcome, objections, and satisfaction signals.

If the request exceeds current scope:

- Do not build a new feature.
- Explain the limitation clearly.
- Offer a narrower manual deliverable if safe.
- Otherwise decline or refund.

## Success Metrics

Use directional metrics rather than vanity metrics.

Minimum useful signals:

- **Reply rate**: At least 8-12% from qualified personalized outreach.
- **Positive reply rate**: At least 3-5% of contacted leads.
- **Free check requests**: At least 5 qualified requests.
- **Paid intent**: At least 2 strong paid-intent conversations or 1 paid pack request.
- **Problem language**: Leads describe buyer review, procurement, security questionnaires, trust pages, AI governance, or sales friction in their own words.

Strong validation signals:

- Leads forward internal context or buyer questionnaires.
- Leads ask about delivery time, price, examples, or what is included.
- Leads compare TrustFolder to internal sales enablement or compliance prep.
- Agencies ask whether packs can help their own clients.
- Leads request a concrete next step without heavy education.

Weak validation signals:

- Replies are polite but vague.
- Leads say it is interesting but not urgent.
- Leads only want free advice.
- Leads ask for certifications TrustFolder cannot provide.
- Leads misunderstand the product as SOC 2, legal review, or automated compliance.

## Decision Rule

At the end of Phase 4, do not ask whether the idea is liked. Ask whether enough qualified buyers pulled it forward.

Possible decisions:

- **Continue**: Qualified buyers requested free checks and at least one paid path emerged.
- **Narrow**: One segment showed significantly stronger pull than others.
- **Reposition**: Pain exists, but the message or deliverable framing is wrong.
- **Stop**: Low reply quality, no urgency, no paid intent, or unsafe expectations.

The decision must be based on logged buyer behavior, not optimism.
